\begin{abstract}
A robot that improves during deployment must put what it learned somewhere.
Retrieval-augmented frozen vision-language-action (VLA) policies already
accumulate deployment experience without gradient updates, and cross-embodiment
retrieval pools already exist. What has not been shown is a persistent store of
\emph{executable action content}, written by a robot \emph{during its own
deployment}, that a \emph{second deployed robot} reads with \emph{no further
update to its policy}. We ask what an entry must contain for this to be
possible. Each experience is written at four levels of abstraction---a
re-groundable language summary, an object-centric subgoal sequence,
object-anchored end-effector deltas, and a joint-space action chunk---under a
body-agnostic key with a learned alignment map, and every level is decoded into
the reader's own action space before it is injected into the reader's frozen
flow-matching sampler. A fixed-base Franka FR3 and a mobile manipulator both
write to and read from one store, in both directions. We report the
transferability spectrum across the four levels, decompose the cross-body loss
into key-side, retargeting and value-side terms, and report the per-trial
memory-harm rate and the injection rate that make these numbers interpretable.
Admitting a third, previously unseen reader costs \todo{N} paired episodes and
\todo{M} GPU-minutes, and no policy gradient.
\end{abstract}

\noindent\textbf{Contributions.}
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{We quantify what a reader swap costs a retrieval memory, in
threshold-free terms.} We measure retrieval quality for same-body and cross-body
queries under four key constructions, reporting precision@$k$ and ROC-AUC against
a positive-pair definition derived from our own (task, phase) annotation, and
reporting the recall---equivalently, the abstention---that a cross-body reader
must pay to reach the same precision as a same-body reader. We deliberately avoid
arguing from the default similarity gate of any single system: the gate value of
a state-of-the-art same-body retriever is a per-deployment trade-off knob, not a
property of the representation, and its own sensitivity study shows the method is
nearly flat across it.

\item \textbf{We make the transferability of an experience an experimentally
separable variable.} One collection produces four value levels per entry under a
shared key and, crucially, a shared readout interface: every level is decoded
into the reader's action space and injected at the same point of the same frozen
sampler, so that abstraction level is varied while the injection path is held
fixed. We report each level with oracle content and with automatically generated
content, separating what the representation costs from what the content
generator costs, and we compare against a derive-on-read store that materialises
only the lowest level and reconstructs the rest at read time.

\item \textbf{We report a persistent action store that two deployed bodies share,
write and read bidirectionally, with no gradient reaching either policy once
deployed.} Each body's base policy is fine-tuned once, before deployment, on
tasks disjoint from the memory-evaluation tasks; nothing is updated when the
store is admitted or when it grows. We further admit a third, previously unseen
reader body and report its admission cost in paired episodes and GPU-minutes,
alongside the cost of a per-body target-side fine-tune on the same pair. We
report the per-trial memory-harm rate---the fraction of paired trials, at matched
initial state and matched sampler noise, in which the store makes the reader
worse than its memoryless base---together with the injection rate that conditions
it.

\item \textbf{We decompose the cross-body loss and characterise a shared store.}
An oracle ladder attributes the gap between a same-body and a cross-body reader
to three additive terms---retrieval, retargeting, and value inexecutability---
without post-hoc human labelling of failures. On a corpus-seeded store of
\todo{1e4--1e5} entries (\todo{N} episodes) shared across tasks and bodies, we
show that the binding constraint is retrieval precision and cross-body
interference rather than capacity.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Problem Setting}
\label{sec:method:setting}

% >> OPENING SENTENCE --- fixes terminology for the whole paper and draws the
% >> line against [recap] in the same breath.  Do not reorder.
We call the body that produced an entry its \emph{writer} and the body that
consumes it a \emph{reader}; a \emph{reader swap} is the substitution of one for
the other with the store held fixed. Unlike~\cite{recap}, where the pool
embodiment is never deployed, both of our bodies are deployed and each is both a
writer and a reader.

Let $\mathcal{B}=\{b_A,b_B\}$ be two bodies with frozen flow-matching VLA
policies $\pi_b(\mathbf{a}\mid\mathbf{o},\ell)$ emitting chunks
$\mathbf{a}\in\mathbb{R}^{H\times d_b}$. Each $\pi_b$ is fine-tuned once, before
deployment, on a task set $\mathcal{T}_\mathrm{base}$ with
$\mathcal{T}_\mathrm{base}\cap\mathcal{T}_\mathrm{eval}=\emptyset$, and is never
updated again (experiment~E0). The store is
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{M}=\{e_i\}_{i=1}^{N},\qquad
e_i=\bigl(\mathbf{k}_i,\ \{v_i^{(\ell)}\}_{\ell=1}^{4},\ \beta_i,\ c_i,\ t_i\bigr),
\label{eq:entry}
\end{equation}
with $\mathbf{k}_i$ a body-agnostic key, $v_i^{(\ell)}$ the value at abstraction
level $\ell$, $\beta_i$ the writer-body tag, $c_i$ a write-gate calibration
score and $t_i$ a timestamp. Writing is a single step with no gradient,
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{M}\leftarrow\mathcal{M}\cup
\bigl\{\,e(\sigma)\ :\ \sigma\in\mathrm{Seg}(\tau),\ g(\tau)=1\,\bigr\},
\label{eq:write}
\end{equation}
where $\mathrm{Seg}$ segments a rollout into motion segments and $g$ is the write
gate. At read time on body $b$,
\begin{align}
\mathcal{I}&=\mathrm{TopK}\bigl(\mathrm{sim}(\mathbf{q}_b(\mathbf{o}_t),\mathbf{k}_i)\bigr),
\label{eq:retrieve}\\
\hat{\mathbf{a}}_i&=\mathcal{D}_b^{(\ell)}\bigl(v_i^{(\ell)},\mathbf{o}_t\bigr)
\in\mathbb{R}^{H\times d_b},
\label{eq:decode}
\end{align}
where $\mathcal{D}_b^{(\ell)}$ decodes level $\ell$ into the \emph{reader's} action
space. This shared interface is what makes abstraction level separable from
injection path. The prior and its injection follow~\cite{rts} unchanged:
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{a}_\mathrm{elite}=\sum_{i\in\mathcal{I}}w_i\,\hat{\mathbf{a}}_i,
\qquad
\mathbf{x}_{t_0}=(1-t_0)\,\mathbf{a}_\mathrm{elite}+t_0\,\boldsymbol{\epsilon}.
\label{eq:inject}
\end{equation}

% >> This sentence is a pre-emptive concession (R2-B1).  Keep it in the body,
% >> not in a footnote: a ratio of success rates is gameable by abstaining, and
% >> saying so before a reviewer does converts an attack into a design choice.
We report $\mathrm{TR}_\ell=\mathrm{SR}^\mathrm{cross}_\ell/
\mathrm{SR}^\mathrm{same}_\ell$ for context only. A ratio of success rates can be
driven to one by abstaining more often; we therefore state every claim in
absolute success rate against each reader's own memoryless base at matched
initial state, and report the injection rate alongside.

\subsection{What an Entry Is}

signals already present in our corpus; no learned segmenter is used. Mean segment

episodes; we report both units throughout.

The alignment map is trained on weakly paired data---the two bodies performing
the same task, collected independently rather than in correspondence---on a task
set $\mathcal{T}_\mathrm{align}$ disjoint from the evaluation tasks
$\mathcal{T}_\mathrm{eval}$. All main-table numbers use the held-out-task

\emph{only} component trained by gradient descent, has fewer than 2\,M
parameters, and trains in \todo{X} GPU-minutes; that figure is the admission cost
reported in Sec.~\ref{sec:results}.

\subsection{Readout: One Interface, Four Levels}
\label{sec:method:readout}

% >> THE MECHANISM CONTRIBUTION, and the single design decision that rescues the
% >> main experiment.  Rationale (R1-B2): [robomme] measures a 14-point SR swing
% >> from changing the injection mechanism alone (30.68 -> 44.51).  If levels
% >> (1)(2) went through condition-side injection while (3)(4) went through the
% >> sampler, Fig. 4 would be uninterpretable --- abstraction and injection path
% >> would be perfectly confounded.  Collapsing all four onto one path also makes
% >> "adopted unchanged from [rts]" a TRUE statement (R1-B7).
Every level is decoded into the reader's action space before injection: level~(1)
is re-grounded by a frozen VLM in the reader's current image and emits subgoals,
becoming level~(2); level~(2) is instantiated as object-frame waypoints,
becoming level~(3); level~(3) is transformed into the reader's task frame and
solved to a joint-space chunk by differential IK, becoming level~(4); level~(4)
is used directly. All four therefore enter the same aggregation and the same
intermediate-state injection of Eq.~\eqref{eq:inject}, so that the level of
abstraction is varied while the injection path is held fixed.

For same-body reads the highest-fidelity level~(4) is preferred; for cross-body
reads the readout climbs from~(4) upward until decoding succeeds and the result
is kinematically feasible. \emph{Which level it lands on is itself an
experimental reading.}

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{Deployment loop for reader body $b$ (policy frozen)}
\label{alg:deploy}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\REQUIRE store $\mathcal{M}$, frozen policy $\pi_b$, key encoder $q_b$,
         alignment map $h_b$
\FOR{each control step $t$ at a chunk boundary}
  \STATE $k_q \leftarrow h_b(q_b(o_t))$
  \STATE $\mathcal{I} \leftarrow \mathrm{TopK}_i\,\mathrm{sim}(k_q,k_i)$;
         filter by DTW consistency \hfill\COMMENT{\cite{rts}}
  \IF{$\mathcal{I}=\emptyset$}
    \STATE $a \leftarrow \pi_b(o_t,\ell)$; \textbf{continue}
           \hfill\COMMENT{abstain}
  \ENDIF
  \STATE $\ell_i \leftarrow$ highest executable level;
         $\hat a_i \leftarrow \mathcal{D}_b^{(\ell_i)}(v_i,o_t)$
  \STATE drop $i$ whose retarget/IK fails; abstain if none remain
  \STATE $a_\mathrm{elite}\leftarrow\sum_i w_i \hat a_i$;
         $c \leftarrow \alpha\tilde{s}-\beta\sigma_\mathrm{DTW}$
  \STATE $t_0 \leftarrow t_{\min}+(1-t_{\min})\,\sigma(-\gamma c)$
  \STATE $a \leftarrow \mathrm{FlowSample}(\pi_b,o_t,\ell,x_{t_0})$
  \STATE execute $a$; buffer $(k_q,\text{executed chunk})$
\ENDFOR
\STATE \textbf{at episode end:} $s\leftarrow\mathrm{WriteGate}(\text{rollout})$
\IF{$s\geq\eta$}
  \STATE $\mathcal{M}\leftarrow\mathcal{M}\cup
         \{e(\sigma,\text{tag}=b)\}_{\sigma\in\mathrm{Seg}(\text{prefix})}$
\ENDIF
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}
